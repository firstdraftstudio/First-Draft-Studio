Service & Delivery Policy

First Draft Studio · Last updated 9 October 2026

From the first conversation to launch and handover, this policy describes the usual project flow. Your accepted proposal should confirm the exact deliverables and responsibilities.

1. Start with an inquiry

Submit the public website inquiry form with your contact details, business information, the kind of website you need and any goals or features you have in mind. You may also create an account to use the account-based inquiry and project features. An inquiry is a request to discuss a project, not an automatic purchase or confirmed booking.

2. Requirements and proposal

We discuss the project's purpose, audience, pages, content, design direction, functionality, integrations, accessibility and mobile requirements. We then confirm the proposed scope, price, estimated milestones, revision process, payment terms, domain and hosting responsibilities, and handover arrangements in writing. Work should not begin until the required details and any agreed prerequisites are confirmed.

3. When work begins

Work begins after the client accepts the proposal and completes any agreed advance payment or provides required content and access. The specific start condition must be stated in the proposal. If requirements or assets are missing, the start date or schedule may need to change.

4. Previews, revisions and approval

We share previews or staging links when included in the project plan. The client should review each milestone and send consolidated, specific feedback through the agreed communication route. The proposal defines the included revision rounds and how out-of-scope changes are quoted. The client should provide explicit approval before launch or final handover. Approval does not remove rights relating to defects or legal protections that cannot be excluded.

5. Informational website delivery

For an informational or brochure-style website, delivery may include the agreed pages, responsive layouts, navigation, contact links or forms specified in the proposal, and deployment to the agreed hosting destination. A form, email delivery, analytics, search indexing, custom integrations or other functionality is included only if explicitly scoped and tested as part of the project.

6. Ecommerce website delivery

For an online store, delivery depends on the accepted scope and may include product pages, catalogues, cart or checkout flows, order records, and administrative features. Payment processing, shipping calculations, inventory sync, tax calculations, notifications, customer accounts and integrations are separate requirements unless specifically included. Live payment or shipping functionality depends on third-party accounts, credentials, eligibility and provider approval. Test orders and operational checks should be agreed before launch.

7. Domain, hosting and launch

The proposal must state whether domain registration, hosting and deployment are included, who owns and controls the accounts, the period covered by any included service, and who pays renewal or ongoing provider charges. The client is responsible for renewing accounts assigned to them and keeping account/payment details current. No particular domain period, hosting period, uptime guarantee or maintenance plan is promised unless written in the proposal.

8. Handover and ongoing maintenance

Handover items may include the live URL, agreed administrator access, source files or repository access, setup notes, and any agreed credentials or documentation. The exact list must be confirmed in writing. Ongoing content updates, security patches, backups, troubleshooting, monitoring and feature changes are not automatically included after handover unless a maintenance arrangement says otherwise.

9. Delays and changes

We will communicate material delays or blockers through the agreed contact channel and explain any change to the expected schedule. Client delays in providing content, approvals or account access, third-party review, provider outages and changes in requirements may affect delivery. If the scope changes, the parties should agree the impact on price and timeline before the additional work proceeds.

10. Questions or concerns

Use the Contact & Grievance page or, for an active account-based project, the account inquiry area. Keep copies of the accepted proposal and approval messages so that both sides can refer to the same scope.
