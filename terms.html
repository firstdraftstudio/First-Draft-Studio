First Draft Studio · Last updated 9 October 2026

Clear expectations help a website project run smoothly. These terms describe the service framework; the final scope and commercial details must be confirmed in a written project proposal.

1. About the service

First Draft Studio offers website planning, design and development services for businesses. The exact deliverables depend on the proposal or written project confirmation agreed with the client. A website inquiry, account registration, displayed package or discussion does not by itself create a binding order or guarantee availability.

2. Packages, prices and optional features

The website displays indicative starting packages of ₹3,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 and ₹20,000. These are starting points, not a guarantee that every feature or requirement is included at that price. Final scope, number of pages, integrations, content, online ordering, ecommerce functionality, support, domain, hosting, taxes if applicable, and delivery expectations must be stated in the proposal before the client accepts.

Optional functionality, including online ordering, payment integration, product/catalogue management, or third-party integrations, may involve additional cost and separate provider charges. No optional feature should be assumed included unless it is recorded in the accepted scope.

3. Project requirements and client responsibilities

The client is responsible for providing accurate business details, timely feedback, approved text, images, logos, product data, pricing, policies, and other materials required for the project. The client must have the rights and permissions needed to use materials they supply, and must ensure that their business claims, products, and practices comply with applicable law. We may ask for clarification where information is incomplete or inconsistent.

4. Timelines, revisions and approval

Work begins after the scope is confirmed and any agreed advance or prerequisites have been completed. The project proposal should specify estimated milestones, what counts as a revision, how feedback is submitted, and what happens if content or approvals are delayed. Timelines are estimates unless expressly agreed as binding. Requests that materially change the scope may require a revised quote and schedule.

Clients should review previews carefully and report issues before approving launch or handover. Approval confirms the client has reviewed the relevant deliverable, but does not remove rights or remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded.

5. Payments

Payment terms require owner confirmation and must be written into each proposal before acceptance. The proposal should state the total fee, any advance or milestone payments, due dates, accepted payment methods, applicable taxes if any, and consequences of late or missed payments. Do not assume a deposit percentage, payment schedule, late fee or tax treatment unless it is explicitly confirmed in writing.

6. Domains, hosting and renewals

Domain registration, hosting, email, premium plugins, payment gateways and other third-party services may have separate costs, renewal dates and terms. Before work begins, the proposal must identify which party purchases and owns each account, who pays recurring fees, who controls DNS and credentials, what is included in any stated hosting period, and who is responsible for renewal. Domain or hosting inclusion must not be assumed from the package price unless written in the proposal.

7. Intellectual property and handover

The proposal should identify ownership and permitted use of the final custom design, source code, content, images, fonts, templates, libraries and third-party components. Unless expressly agreed, no transfer of third-party intellectual property is implied. Client-supplied materials remain subject to the client's rights and any applicable licence. Any handover of source files, administrator access, credentials, repository access or documentation should be listed in the agreed deliverables. We will not knowingly transfer credentials or assets that we do not have the right to transfer.

8. Third-party services and technical limitations

Websites may depend on providers such as hosting platforms, domain registrars, Supabase, payment gateways, email services, APIs, or software libraries. Those services are subject to their own terms, charges, limits, availability and changes. We cannot guarantee uninterrupted operation of services controlled by third parties. Any integrations, security requirements, backups, maintenance and support commitments should be documented in the project scope.

9. Cancellation, refunds and concerns

Cancellation and refund requests are assessed under the accepted project agreement, the work actually completed, approved deliverables, committed third-party costs, and applicable law. No blanket non-refundable rule is created by this page. Please see the Refund and Cancellation Policy for the request and review process. Any specific refund timetable or formula must be confirmed by the owner and stated in the accepted proposal.

10. Liability and disputes

We will use reasonable care in delivering the work agreed in writing. To the extent permitted by applicable law, neither party should be responsible for losses caused by the other party's inaccurate materials, unauthorized changes, misuse, or third-party outages beyond its reasonable control. Nothing in these terms excludes or limits liability, consumer rights, or remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded or limited. Any project-specific liability terms must be fair, clear and reviewed for applicability before use.

If a concern arises, the parties should first try to resolve it through the contact route below and preserve relevant project communications. Applicable law and dispute-resolution arrangements should be stated in the accepted project agreement where appropriate; this draft does not invent a court location or arbitration commitment.

11. Changes and contact

These terms may be updated for future projects. The version and project proposal accepted by the client should be retained. For questions, cancellations or concerns, use the Contact & Grievance page or the website's inquiry section.
